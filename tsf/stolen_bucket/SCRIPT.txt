In 1325, two Italian cities went to war. The winner's prize? A wooden bucket.
Modena and Bologna were neighbours, about forty kilometres apart. And they hated each other. Bologna backed the Pope. Modena backed the Emperor.
That November, Bologna marched out with around thirty thousand soldiers. Modena had about seven thousand.
Easy win. It wasn't.
Near a castle called Zappolino, the Modenese charged. In about two hours, Bologna's giant army broke and ran. All the way home.
The Modenese chased them right up to the city gates. They could have taken gold. Prisoners. Maybe the whole city.
Instead, they walked up to a well. And stole the bucket.
Then, the story goes, they held horse races outside Bologna's walls. Just to rub it in.
Around two thousand men died that day. And the war changed almost nothing.
But the bucket went home to Modena as a trophy. Three hundred years later, a poet wrote a whole comic epic about it, called The Stolen Bucket.
Bologna never got it back. It's still in Modena today.
Seven centuries later, it's the most famous bucket in Italy.
